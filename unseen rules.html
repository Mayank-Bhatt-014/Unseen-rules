How much of your life is decided by unwritten gender rules?

Nobody handed you a rulebook, yet most of us can recite it: where to sit, when to be home, what to study, who cooks, who pays, who may cry. These rules shape women, men and everyone in between. This observatory asks IGDTUW students to look at them together, and to record what they see.

Why we are doing this

Rules nobody wrote down are the hardest to question.

Gender norms rarely arrive as written rules. They arrive as advice, jokes, curfews and expectations about who earns, who cooks, who may cry and who must be home before dark. Because they are unwritten, they are easy to mistake for "just how things are".

This observatory has three aims: to make those rules visible; to document which ones IGDTUW students say they see, for women, for men and for everyone; and to turn what we learn into practical suggestions for the campus and community. It supports the UN Sustainable Development Goal 5, Gender Equality.

Gender equality is not a women's issue alone. Rules that tell men to hide distress or carry every bill harm them and the people who love them. Rules that limit women's choices, freedom and safety harm everyone. We ask about both.

What we hope you take away

  • Naming a rule is the first step to questioning it.
  • Many rules limit people of every gender, in different ways.
  • A fair response changes the situation, not only the person in it.

A note on who is answering: respondents are students of a women's university, so what you report about men and boys is what you observe around you, not their own account. The report says so, and recommends bringing male students' voices in directly in the next round.

Privacy and care

This page stores no name, email or device identifier with your answers. Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at any point. If a statement brings up something difficult, pause. In India, 112 is the emergency number and 181 is the Women Helpline. Your university's counselling service and Internal Committee can also help.
